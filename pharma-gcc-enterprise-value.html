From Execution to Enterprise Value: The Next Evolution of Pharma GCCs

Global capability centers in life sciences can move beyond delivery scale by building differentiated capabilities, staying close to business outcomes and creating measurable enterprise value.
By Mohammad Mujeeb Beg · Perspective

Global capability centers have evolved significantly. What began in many organizations as a way to access talent and improve delivery efficiency can become something much more strategic: an enterprise capability hub that owns distinctive products, platforms, data, engineering and AI capabilities.

The question is no longer simply, “How much work can the center take on?” A more useful question is, “What capabilities should the organization deliberately build here because they can create an advantage for the enterprise?”

1. Move from capacity to capability

Capacity is measured in people, tickets and throughput. Capability is measured in what the organization can uniquely do, improve and scale. A strong GCC develops deep expertise in areas that matter to the enterprise—commercial technology, data, product engineering, AI, customer engagement or other strategic domains.

2. Put the business problem at the center

Technology teams create more value when they work close to the business problem. Product managers, engineers, analysts and business stakeholders should share a common understanding of the outcome being pursued. This changes the conversation from delivery commitments to business impact.

“A GCC becomes strategic when it is trusted to own outcomes, not just execute requests.”

3. Measure enterprise value

Value can show up in faster launches, better customer experiences, higher productivity, improved quality, stronger data foundations or new digital capabilities. The metric should reflect the business outcome—not only the activity performed.

4. Design for scale

Scale requires more than hiring. It requires clear product ownership, engineering standards, talent development, reusable platforms, strong governance and leaders who can translate enterprise strategy into capability roadmaps.

The next generation of pharma GCCs will increasingly be defined by what they create and own. The opportunity is to build organizations that are not peripheral delivery engines, but strategic partners that help the enterprise compete, innovate and serve patients better.
